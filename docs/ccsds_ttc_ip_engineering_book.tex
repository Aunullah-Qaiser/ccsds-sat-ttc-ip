\partbanner{Part I}{System Architecture \& Multi-Clock Transponder Theory}

\section{Architectural Principles of Autonomous Spacecraft TT\&C Transceivers}

Unlike terrestrial wireless modems that optimize solely for peak spectral efficiency under continuous microprocessor supervision, an orbital spacecraft Telemetry, Tracking, and Command (TT\&C) radio serves as the vehicle's non-negotiable survival lifeline. This opening chapter formulates the five physical and operational invariants that govern \texttt{CCSDS-SAT-TTC-IP} (\texttt{ccsds\_sat\_ttc\_top v2.40.3}), proves why the dual-persona architecture eliminates flight-to-ground protocol divergence, and derives the mathematical coupling across the three closed-loop transponder feedback paths.

\subsection{Five Foundational Invariants of Orbital Transceiver Design}
Across Low-Earth Orbit (LEO), geostationary (GEO), cislunar, and deep-space missions, \texttt{ccsds\_sat\_ttc\_top} enforces five architectural invariants:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.5pt}
  \item \textbf{Zero Hard-Macro Vulnerability (\texttt{0 BRAM / 0 DSP}):} In radiation environments, FPGA Block RAMs (\texttt{RAMB36}) and DSP slices (\texttt{DSP48}) exhibit configuration and state Single-Event Functional Interrupts (SEFIs) that cannot be scrubbed without a full device reconfiguration. By realizing all multi-kilobyte CDC FIFOs in synchronous \textbf{Distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{RAM64M}, 1,200 LUTs)} guarded by continuous Hsiao $(39,32)$ SECDED scrubbing and all FIR/NCO arithmetic in Canonical Signed-Digit (CSD) LUT adder trees, the entire flight persona consumes \textbf{0 Block RAMs, 0 DSP slices, and 13,864 Slice Flip-Flops}.
  \item \textbf{Autonomous Command Recovery Without Software Execution:} During a solar proton event or OBC watchdog hang, the system bus clock (\texttt{clk\_sys}) may stall. Clocking the entire uplink telecommand chain—from soft-LLR ASM synchronization and $\text{BCH}(63,56)$/$\text{Short-LDPC}(128,64)$ decoding down to the 3x TMR Emergency Hardware Command Pulse Decoder (\texttt{HCPD}, \texttt{VCID=63})—from an independent $16\,\text{MHz}$ crystal oscillator (\texttt{clk\_tc\_safe}) controlled by hardware mission straps (\texttt{ul\_fec\_override\_en[1:0]}) guarantees commandability with zero CPU intervention.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Protocol Unification:} Converges all five CCSDS Space Data Link protocols—\texttt{TC} \cite{ccsds232}, \texttt{TM} \cite{ccsds132}, \texttt{AOS} \cite{ccsds732}, \texttt{Proximity-1} \cite{ccsds211}, and \texttt{USLP} \cite{ccsds7321}—within a single datapath.
  \item \textbf{Zero-Trust Cryptographic Atomicity:} Prevents the \textbf{\texttt{FARM-1}/\texttt{SDLS} Sequence-Lockout Paradox}—where an unauthenticated frame with a forged sequence number $N(S) = V(R)$ and valid CRC-16 advances $V(R)$ before SDLS MAC verification—by splitting \texttt{FARM-1} window checking (Phase~A) and atomic $V(R)$ commit (Phase~B) across a swipe-proof speculative rollback FIFO.
  \item \textbf{Dual-Persona Mathematical Symmetry (\texttt{ROLE\_MODE = 0, 1, 2}):} Unites the Spacecraft Flight transceiver (\texttt{ROLE\_MODE = 0}, 23 modules), Hardware Ground EGSE (\texttt{ROLE\_MODE = 1}, 22 modules), and Universal Crosslink/BIST transceiver (\texttt{ROLE\_MODE = 2}, 28 modules) in a single parameterized hierarchy sharing 78.6\% of the RTL modules.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation of the Three Coupled Transponder Loops}
Referring to the top-level architecture and signal-flow diagrams in Volume~I (\textbf{Figures~1.1--1.2}), three real-time feedback loops couple the uplink receiver to the downlink transmitter:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.5pt}
  \item \textbf{Loop \#1 --- Atomic 4-Phase Toggle Handshake for Closed-Loop COP-1 (\texttt{FARM-1} $\to$ \texttt{CLCW} $\to$ \texttt{FOP-1}):} In the $16\,\text{MHz}$ \texttt{clk\_tc\_safe} domain, \texttt{ccsds\_farm1\_tmr.sv} maintains the triplicated receiver sequence counter $V(R) \in [0..255]$ and formats the 32-bit \texttt{CLCW}. Because the downlink framer resides in \texttt{clk\_sys} ($100\,\text{MHz}$), the 32-bit \texttt{CLCW} word crosses via a 4-phase Request/Acknowledge toggle handshake (\texttt{clcw\_req\_tgl} / \texttt{clcw\_ack\_tgl} synchronized through 2-FF \texttt{ASYNC\_REG} stages), guaranteeing that a multi-bit word is never sampled mid-transition.
  \item \textbf{Loop \#2 --- Regenerative \texttt{T2B}/\texttt{T4B} Pseudo-Noise Ranging Turnaround:} Per CCSDS 414.1-B-3 \cite{ccsds414}, the composite ranging sequence of period $L = 2 \times 7 \times 11 \times 15 \times 19 \times 23 = 1{,}009{,}470$ chips is acquired in \texttt{ccsds\_pn\_ranging.sv} and regenerated noise-free onto the downlink quadrature channel, eliminating the thermal-noise turnaround loss of analog bent-pipe transponders.
  \item \textbf{Loop \#3 --- Fixed-Point Q32 Coherent Turnaround Frequency Synthesis (S, X, X$\to$Ka, and Ka$\to$Ka):} Per CCSDS 401.0-B-32 \cite{ccsds401} (Tables 2.4-1 and 2.5-1), two-way coherent Doppler tracking requires multiplying the tracked uplink carrier frequency $f_{\text{ul}}$ by an exact rational turnaround ratio $M/N$ to synthesize the downlink carrier $f_{\text{dl}} = (M/N) f_{\text{ul}}$. It is essential to distinguish between \textbf{intra-band} turnaround ratios and \textbf{X-to-Ka cross-band} turnaround ratios:
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
    \item \emph{S-Band Uplink ($2025\text{--}2120\,\text{MHz}$) $\to$ S-Band Downlink ($2200\text{--}2300\,\text{MHz}$):} $M/N = 240/221 \approx 1.085973$.
    \item \emph{X-Band Uplink ($7145\text{--}7235\,\text{MHz}$) $\to$ X-Band Downlink ($8400\text{--}8500\,\text{MHz}$):} $M/N = 880/749 \approx 1.174900$.
    \item \emph{X-Band Uplink ($7145\text{--}7235\,\text{MHz}$) $\to$ Ka-Band Downlink ($31.8\text{--}32.3\,\text{GHz}$) Cross-Band:} $M/N = 3344/749 \approx 4.464619$ (Category~B Deep Space) and $M/N = 3360/749 \approx 4.485981$ or $3328/749 \approx 4.443258$ (Category~A Near-Earth).
    \item \emph{Ka-Band Uplink ($34.2\text{--}34.7\,\text{GHz}$) $\to$ Ka-Band Downlink ($31.8\text{--}32.3\,\text{GHz}$) Intra-Band:} $M/N = 3344/3599 \approx 0.929147$ (Category~B) or $3328/3599 \approx 0.924701$ (Category~A).
  \end{itemize}
  In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_phy\_modem.sv} (lines 380--386), the tracked 32-bit uplink NCO Frequency Control Word ($\text{FCW}_{\text{rx}}$) is scaled without combinational division using pre-computed Q32 fixed-point reciprocal multipliers $K_{\text{Q32}}(M/N) = \lfloor (M/N) \cdot 2^{32} + 0.5 \rfloor$:
  \begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \text{FCW}_{\text{tx}} &= \left(\text{FCW}_{\text{rx}} \times K_{\text{Q32}}(M/N)\right) \gg 32, \\
    K_{\text{Q32}}(240/221) &= \texttt{64'h0000\_0001\_1602\_5117}, \qquad K_{\text{Q32}}(880/749)  = \texttt{64'h0000\_0001\_2CC6\_7C21}, \\
    K_{\text{Q32}}(3344/749) &= \texttt{64'h0000\_0004\_76F0\_D8FC}, \qquad K_{\text{Q32}}(3344/3599) = \texttt{64'h0000\_0000\_EDDD\_718B},
  \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  achieving $< 2.3 \times 10^{-10}$ relative frequency accuracy in a single pipelined register stage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Finite-Field Algebra, Spectral Randomization \& Fault-Tolerant Memory}

Before a space link can frame packets or correct channel errors, it requires a rigorous foundation in finite-field algebra ($\mathbb{F}_2[x]$, $GF(2^4)$, and $GF(2^8)$) and radiation-tolerant storage. This chapter derives the complete 16-bit unrolled parallel transition equations for CRC-16-CCITT, formulates the additive pseudo-randomizers and Berlekamp dual-basis bijection, derives the shortened $GF(2^4)$ $\text{RS}(10,6)$ AOS header protector, and proves how our Hsiao $(39,32)$ SECDED Distributed-LUTRAM FIFOs guarantee swipe-proof speculative rollback.

\subsection{Parallel Companion Matrix Derivation for CRC-16 and CRC-32}
Frame error detection across all five CCSDS Space Data Link protocols relies on polynomial division over $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$ implemented in \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_crc\_universal.sv}:
\begin{align}
  G_{16}(x) &= x^{16} + x^{12} + x^5 + 1 \quad (\texttt{16'h1021},\; \text{seed } \texttt{16'hFFFF},\; \text{valid residue } \texttt{16'h0000 / 16'h1D0F}), \\
  G_{32}(x) &= x^{32} + x^{23} + x^{21} + x^{11} + x^2 + 1 \quad (\texttt{32'h00A0\_0805},\; \text{seed } \texttt{32'hFFFF\_FFFF}).
\end{align}
Let $c_{\text{in}}[15:0]$ denote the current 16-bit CRC-16 register and $d[7:0]$ denote the incoming AXI4-Stream byte (processed MSB-first from $b=7$ down to $b=0$ as in \texttt{step\_crc16\_byte}). Define the 8-bit upper XOR vector $x_i = c_{\text{in}}[8+i] \oplus d[i]$ for $i \in [0..7]$. Why do the lower 8 incoming state bits $c_{\text{in}}[7:0]$ appear only in $c_{\text{out}}[15:8]$ and never in $c_{\text{out}}[7:0]$? Because each of the 8 single-bit left shifts moves $c_{\text{in}}[7:0]$ up by 8 bit positions into $c_{\text{out}}[15:8]$ without ever reaching the MSB feedback tap $c[15]$ during that byte! Only $c_{\text{in}}[15:8] \oplus d[7:0] = x[7:0]$ crosses the feedback tap during the 8 shifts, with the $x^{12}$ tap coupling $x_7..x_4$ back into $x_3..x_0$. Unrolling all 8 shifts yields the complete, exact 16-bit closed-form XOR transition equations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  c_{\text{out}}[0]  &= x_0 \oplus x_4,                  & c_{\text{out}}[8]  &= c_{\text{in}}[0] \oplus x_3 \oplus x_7, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[1]  &= x_1 \oplus x_5,                  & c_{\text{out}}[9]  &= c_{\text{in}}[1] \oplus x_4, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[2]  &= x_2 \oplus x_6,                  & c_{\text{out}}[10] &= c_{\text{in}}[2] \oplus x_5, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[3]  &= x_3 \oplus x_7,                  & c_{\text{out}}[11] &= c_{\text{in}}[3] \oplus x_6, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[4]  &= x_4,                             & c_{\text{out}}[12] &= c_{\text{in}}[4] \oplus x_0 \oplus x_4 \oplus x_7, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[5]  &= x_0 \oplus x_4 \oplus x_5,       & c_{\text{out}}[13] &= c_{\text{in}}[5] \oplus x_1 \oplus x_5, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[6]  &= x_1 \oplus x_5 \oplus x_6,       & c_{\text{out}}[14] &= c_{\text{in}}[6] \oplus x_2 \oplus x_6, \\
  c_{\text{out}}[7]  &= x_2 \oplus x_6 \oplus x_7,       & c_{\text{out}}[15] &= c_{\text{in}}[7] \oplus x_3 \oplus x_7.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every bit of $c_{\text{out}}[15:0]$ is a function of at most 4 XOR inputs, evaluating in a \textbf{single 6-input LUT delay} per byte and producing the exact CCSDS standard check value $\text{CRC16}(\texttt{"123456789"}) = \texttt{16'h29B1}$.

\subsection{Telemetry and Telecommand Pseudo-Randomizers}
To guarantee sufficient symbol-transition density for Gardner timing recovery and disperse periodic spectral lines, \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_lfsr\_scrambler.sv} implements both CCSDS additive (synchronous) pseudo-randomizers over period $2^8 - 1 = 255$ bits (\cite{ccsds131,ccsds231}):
\begin{align}
  h_{\text{TM}}(x) &= x^8 + x^7 + x^5 + x^3 + 1 \quad (\text{tap mask } \texttt{8'hA9},\; \text{init } \texttt{8'hFF} \implies \texttt{FF 48 0E C0 9A}\dots), \\
  h_{\text{TC}}(x) &= x^8 + x^6 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 \quad (\text{tap mask } \texttt{8'h5F},\; \text{init } \texttt{8'hFF} \implies \texttt{FF 39 9E 5A 68}\dots).
\end{align}
Both generators initialize to all-ones (\texttt{8'hFF}) immediately following the unscrambled Attached Sync Marker (ASM) and step 8 LFSR states per clock cycle to XOR-scramble or de-scramble one full byte per cycle.

\subsection{Galois Field $GF(2^8)$ \& Berlekamp Dual-Basis Bijection}
Per CCSDS 131.0-B-5 \cite{ccsds131}, downlink Reed-Solomon $\text{RS}(255,223)$ codewords are constructed over the finite field $GF(2^8) \cong \mathbb{F}_2[x]/\langle F(x)\rangle$ defined by the primitive polynomial:
\begin{equation}
  F(x) = x^8 + x^7 + x^2 + x + 1 \quad (\texttt{9'h187}), \qquad F(\alpha) = 0 \implies \alpha^8 = \alpha^7 + \alpha^2 + \alpha + 1.
\end{equation}
While internal syndrome, Berlekamp-Massey, Chien, and Forney arithmetic is naturally expressed in the \textbf{standard polynomial basis} $\{\alpha^7, \alpha^6, \dots, \alpha^1, 1\}$ where $u \in GF(2^8)$ has coordinate vector $\mathbf{u} = [u_7, u_6, \dots, u_0]^T$, CCSDS 131.0-B-5 Annex~B mandates that symbols on the space link be represented in \textbf{Berlekamp's dual basis} $\{z_0, z_1, \dots, z_7\}$ orthogonal to $\{\ell_0, \dots, \ell_7\} = \{1, \alpha, \dots, \alpha^7\}$ under the trace bilinear form $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma \beta_i \ell_j) = \delta_{ij}$. In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_pkg.sv} and \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_gf256\_math.sv}, the change-of-basis transformation $\mathbf{z} = T_\alpha \mathbf{u} \pmod 2$ and its exact inverse $\mathbf{u} = T_\alpha^{-1}\mathbf{z} \pmod 2$ ($T_\alpha T_\alpha^{-1} = I_8$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$) are realized as zero-latency 8-bit XOR matrix-vector products:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  T_\alpha &= \big[\texttt{0xFE},\; \texttt{0x69},\; \texttt{0x6B},\; \texttt{0x0D},\; \texttt{0xEF},\; \texttt{0xF2},\; \texttt{0x5B},\; \texttt{0xC7}\big]^T \quad (\alpha^0 \mapsto \texttt{0x7B},\; \alpha^7 \mapsto \texttt{0x8D}), \\
  T_\alpha^{-1} &= \big[\texttt{0x9B},\; \texttt{0xDD},\; \texttt{0x3E},\; \texttt{0x1C},\; \texttt{0x66},\; \texttt{0x33},\; \texttt{0xA9},\; \texttt{0x2D}\big]^T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Shortened $\text{RS}(10,6)$ over $GF(2^4)$ for AOS Header Protection}
In CCSDS 732.0-B-4 AOS frames \cite{ccsds732}, the 24 protected header bits (\texttt{TFVN}, \texttt{SCID}, \texttt{VCID}, \texttt{Replay}, \texttt{Usage}, \texttt{Rsvd}, and \texttt{VC\_Cycle}) are partitioned into six 4-bit nibbles $m_5, m_4, \dots, m_0 \in GF(2^4)$ modulo $p(x) = x^4 + x + 1$ (\texttt{5'h13}) and encoded with a shortened $\text{RS}(10,6)$ code of minimum distance $d_{\min} = 5$:
\begin{equation}
  g_{\text{FHEC}}(x) = \prod_{j=6}^{9}(x + \alpha^j) = x^4 + \alpha^3 x^3 + \alpha^1 x^2 + \alpha^3 x + 1 \implies (g_3, g_2, g_1, g_0) = (\texttt{4'h8},\; \texttt{4'h2},\; \texttt{4'h8},\; \texttt{4'h1}).
\end{equation}
On receive, let $S_j = R(\alpha^{6+j}) \in GF(2^4)$ for $j \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ be the four 4-bit syndromes of the 10-nibble header. If a single nibble at index $k \in [0..9]$ is corrupted by error pattern $e \in GF(2^4)\setminus\{0\}$, then $S_0 = e \alpha^{6k}$ and $S_1 = e \alpha^{7k}$, so the ratio $S_1 / S_0 = \alpha^k$ uniquely identifies the corrupted nibble position $k$ while $e = S_0 \alpha^{-6k}$ gives the exact 4-bit error mask—correcting up to 4 consecutive bit flips in a nibble in a single clock cycle.

\subsection{Odd-Weight Hsiao $(39,32)$ SECDED LUTRAM FIFOs \& Speculative Rollback}
In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_cdc\_fifo\_ecc.sv} and the 64-entry SDLS Security Association RAM, every 32-bit word $D[31:0]$ is encoded with a 7-bit \textbf{Hsiao $(39,32)$ SECDED} parity vector $P[6:0]$ constructed from odd-weight parity masks:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  H_{\text{Hsiao}} &= \big[\texttt{32'h56AA\_AD5B},\; \texttt{32'h9B33\_366D},\; \texttt{32'hE3C3\_C78E}, \\
                   &\phantom{=\big[}\texttt{32'h03FC\_07F0},\; \texttt{32'h03FF\_F800},\; \texttt{32'hFC00\_0000}\big]^T, \\
  P[5:0] &= H_{\text{Hsiao}}\,D[31:0] \pmod 2, \qquad P[6] = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{31} D[i] \oplus \bigoplus_{j=0}^{5} P[j].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because the storage array \texttt{mem\_ecc\_reg} is written inside a dedicated synchronous process without asynchronous reset sensitivity, synthesis maps all FIFO storage into \textbf{Distributed LUTRAMs (\texttt{RAM64M}, 1,200 LUTs across the chip)} rather than discrete Flip-Flops. To prevent unauthenticated uplink telecommand bytes from leaking across the \texttt{clk\_tc\_safe} $\to$ \texttt{clk\_sys} boundary, \texttt{ccsds\_cdc\_fifo\_ecc.sv} maintains three write-domain pointers: the committed boundary $\text{ptr}_{\text{comm}}$, the active speculative write pointer $\text{ptr}_{\text{spec}}$, and the end-of-frame snapshot $\text{ptr}_{\text{eof}}$ (\texttt{frame\_end\_ptr\_q}). When Frame~$N$ passes CRC-16, $\text{ptr}_{\text{eof}} \leftarrow \text{ptr}_{\text{spec}}$ is latched immediately—even if Frame~$N+1$ begins arriving in the same CLTU while Frame~$N$ is still undergoing SDLS MAC verification. Asserting \texttt{commit\_pulse} updates $\text{ptr}_{\text{comm}} \leftarrow \text{ptr}_{\text{eof}}$, exposing only authenticated Frame~$N$ to the read port, whereas \texttt{rollback\_pulse} rewinds $\text{ptr}_{\text{spec}} \leftarrow \text{ptr}_{\text{comm}}$.

\section{Unified Space Data Link Layer: Cycle-0 \texttt{FHP} \& Multi-Frame CLTU Theory}

While Volume~I, Chapters~3--4 catalog the packed header structures and bitfields for \texttt{TM}, \texttt{TC}, \texttt{AOS}, \texttt{Proximity-1}, and \texttt{USLP}, this chapter derives the two core algorithmic innovations in the framing layer: the \textbf{Cycle-0 closed-form \texttt{First\_Hdr\_Ptr} (\texttt{FHP}) recurrence} and the \textbf{multi-frame CLTU \texttt{0x55} fill-stripping automaton}.

\subsection{Induction Proof of Cycle-0 Closed-Form \texttt{First\_Hdr\_Ptr} (\texttt{FHP}) Calculation}
In continuous downlink protocols (\texttt{TM}, \texttt{AOS}, and \texttt{USLP}), variable-length Space Packets are multiplexed back-to-back across fixed-length Transfer Frame Data Fields (\texttt{TFDF}) of capacity $L_{\text{TFDF}} \in [1..1024]$ bytes. Because the 48-bit TM Primary Header is transmitted \emph{before} the payload zone, the framer must determine on \textbf{Cycle~0} the exact byte offset $\texttt{FHP} \in [0..L_{\text{TFDF}}-1]$ where the first new Space Packet header begins inside that frame.

Rather than buffering an entire frame in Block RAM to scan for packet headers retroactively, \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_vc\_arbiter\_mux.sv} exploits the fact that every CCSDS 133.0-B-2 Space Packet header declares its total length $L_{\text{pkt}} = 6 + (\texttt{pkt\_data\_len} + 1)$ in bytes $4..5$. Let $R_k \ge 0$ (\texttt{rem\_in\_cur\_pkt\_q}) denote the number of residual un-transmitted bytes of an in-progress Space Packet at the start of Frame~$k$ (with $R_0 = 0$ at reset). By induction on $k$:
\begin{equation}
  \texttt{FHP}_k = \begin{cases}
    \texttt{11'h7FE} & \text{if } R_k == 0 \text{ and no Virtual Channel has a valid packet ready (Only-Idle-Data frame)}, \\
    \texttt{11'h000} & \text{if } R_k == 0 \text{ and a selected VC starts a new Space Packet at Byte 0}, \\
    R_k[10:0]        & \text{if } 0 < R_k < L_{\text{TFDF}} \text{ (spillover ends at byte } R_k-1\text{; next packet starts at offset } R_k\text{)}, \\
    \texttt{11'h7FF} & \text{if } R_k \ge L_{\text{TFDF}} \text{ (jumbo packet spans entire frame; } R_{k+1} = R_k - L_{\text{TFDF}}\text{)}.
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Within Frame~$k$, whenever a new Space Packet header is ingested at byte index $b \in [0..L_{\text{TFDF}}-1]$ with declared length $L_{\text{pkt}}$, the residual counter updates via $R_{\text{next}} = \max(0,\; b + L_{\text{pkt}} - L_{\text{TFDF}})$. If a packet finishes at $b + L_{\text{pkt}} < L_{\text{TFDF}}$ and the active VC queue goes empty, the arbiter immediately injects deterministic CCSDS Idle Packets (\texttt{APID = 11'h7FF}) to fill the remainder of $L_{\text{TFDF}}$, guaranteeing that the next packet header starts at the exact byte offset $R_k$ predicted on Cycle~0 with \textbf{zero frame-buffering latency and zero Block RAM}.

\subsection{Multi-Frame CLTU Delimitation \& \texttt{0x55} Fill-Octet Stripping Automaton}
Under CCSDS 231.0-B-4 \cite{ccsds231}, an uplink Command Link Transmission Unit (\texttt{CLTU}) encapsulates one or more variable-length TC or USLP Transfer Frames inside a sequence of 7-octet (56-bit) $\text{BCH}(63,56)$ information blocks terminated by the 64-bit Tail Sequence (\texttt{64'hC5C5\_C5C5\_C5C5\_C579}). When the total Transfer Frame length $L_{\text{exact}} = \texttt{frame\_len} + 1$ is not an integer multiple of 7 octets, the ground encoder (\texttt{ccsds\_bch6356\_enc.sv}) pads the final codeblock with $p = (7 - (L_{\text{exact}} \bmod 7)) \bmod 7 \in [0..6]$ alternating \texttt{8'h55} (\texttt{01010101\_2}) fill octets.

How does the spacecraft deframer (\texttt{ccsds\_tc\_uslp\_deframer.sv}) distinguish between trailing \texttt{0x55} padding octets and a second Transfer Frame concatenated back-to-back inside the same CLTU?
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item During the first 5 octets of a frame (\texttt{ST\_HDR}), the deframer extracts the 10-bit TC \texttt{frame\_len} field (or 16-bit USLP length field) and loads a down-counter $C_{\text{rem}} \leftarrow L_{\text{exact}} - 5$.
  \item On the exact clock cycle where $C_{\text{rem}}$ reaches $0$ (\texttt{ST\_CRC\_CHECK}), the deframer evaluates the 16-bit CRC-16 residue $R_{\text{CRC}} == \texttt{16'h0000}$ and emits \texttt{tlast = 1'b1} to the speculative rollback FIFO.
  \item In the following state (\texttt{ST\_SKIP\_FILL}), if the next decoded octet equals \texttt{8'h55} (or the CLTU tail pulse asserts), the octet is silently discarded as padding; if the octet is non-\texttt{8'h55} (since a valid TC Primary Header starts with $\texttt{TFVN} = \texttt{2'b00} \implies \text{Byte }0 \in [\texttt{0x00}..\texttt{0x3F}] \ne \texttt{0x55}$), the FSM transitions directly back to \texttt{ST\_HDR} to parse the concatenated frame without losing a single byte.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Forward Error Correction: Algebraic Block Codes to Iterative Soft-Decision Decoders}

This chapter derives the mathematical formulations of the five Forward Error Correction (FEC) engines in \texttt{ccsds\_sat\_ttc\_top}, progressing from phase-ambiguous soft-LLR synchronization and algebraic $\text{BCH}(63,56)$ syndrome decoding to our 4-phase time-multiplexed $\text{Short-LDPC}(128,64)$ Soft-OMS iterative decoder and 8-cycle unrolled $t=16$ Berlekamp-Massey $\text{RS}(255,223)$ decoder.

\subsection{Differential Soft-LLR ASM Correlation Under $0^\circ/180^\circ$ Phase Ambiguity}
Because suppressed-carrier BPSK Costas loops exhibit a two-fold ($0^\circ / 180^\circ$) phase lock ambiguity, the demodulated 4-bit signed Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) stream $\ell_k \in [-8, +7]$ may arrive inverted. In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_asm\_correlator.sv}, a sliding window of $M \in \{16, 24, 32, 64\}$ soft LLRs is correlated simultaneously against both the true Attached Sync Marker pattern $p_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ (\texttt{16'hEB90} for CLTU, \texttt{32'h1ACFFC1D} for TM/AOS, \texttt{24'hFAF320} for Proximity-1, and \texttt{64'h034776C7272895B0} for Short-LDPC/AR4JA) and its bitwise complement:
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\text{corr}} = \sum_{i=0}^{M-1} p_i\,\operatorname{sgn}(\ell_{k-i}), \qquad
  \text{Lock} \iff |\Gamma_{\text{corr}}| \ge \Gamma_{\text{thresh}}, \qquad
  \text{Invert} \iff \Gamma_{\text{corr}} \le -\Gamma_{\text{thresh}}.
\end{equation}
When inverted lock is detected, the correlator automatically negates all subsequent LLRs ($\ell_k \leftarrow -\ell_k$) across the frame.

\subsection{Uplink Expurgated $\text{BCH}(63,56)$ SEC-DED Codec}
Per CCSDS 231.0-B-4 \cite{ccsds231}, each 64-bit uplink CLTU codeblock contains 56 information bits $m(x)$, 7 parity bits $r(x) = \overline{(x^7 m(x) \bmod g_{\text{BCH}}(x))}$ generated by the expurgated polynomial $g_{\text{BCH}}(x) = (x^6 + x + 1)(x + 1) = x^7 + x^6 + x^2 + 1$ (\texttt{7'h45}), and 1 trailing filler bit (\texttt{0}). In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_bch6356\_dec.sv}, the 7-bit syndrome $s(x) = c_{62..0}(x) \bmod g_{\text{BCH}}(x)$ is decomposed into the 6-bit primitive syndrome $s_6 \in GF(2^6)$ and overall codeblock parity $p_{\text{odd}} = s(1)$:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item $s_6 == 0 \land p_{\text{odd}} == 0$: Codeblock is error-free; all 7 de-randomized information octets are emitted.
  \item $s_6 \ne 0 \land p_{\text{odd}} == 1$: Single-bit error detected; a 63-entry discrete-logarithm lookup table maps $s_6 = \alpha^k$ to the exact corrupted bit index $k \in [0..62]$, healing the bit in hardware and incrementing \texttt{cnt\_secded\_corr\_q}.
  \item $s_6 \ne 0 \land p_{\text{odd}} == 0$: Uncorrectable double-bit error or intentional CLTU Tail Sequence (\texttt{64'hC5C5\_C5C5\_C5C5\_C579}, which produces the deterministic tail syndrome \texttt{7'h25}), cleanly terminating CLTU reception.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Uplink 4-Phase Time-Multiplexed $\text{Short-LDPC}(128,64)$ Soft-OMS Decoder}
For low-SNR safe-mode links and deep-space telecommanding, CCSDS 231.1-O-1 \cite{ccsds2311} specifies a rate-$1/2$ $(128,64)$ quasi-cyclic Low-Density Parity-Check (LDPC) code built from $16 \times 16$ circulant submatrices $I_s$ (the $16\times 16$ identity matrix circularly shifted right by $s$ columns) and $\mathbf{0}$:
\begin{equation}
  H_{64 \times 128} = \begin{bmatrix}
    I_0 \oplus I_7 & I_2 & I_{14} & I_6 & \mathbf{0} & I_0 & I_{13} & I_0 \\
    I_6 & I_0 \oplus I_{15} & I_0 & I_1 & I_0 & \mathbf{0} & I_0 & I_7 \\
    I_4 & I_1 & I_0 \oplus I_{15} & I_{14} & I_{11} & I_0 & \mathbf{0} & I_3 \\
    I_0 & I_1 & I_9 & I_0 \oplus I_{13} & I_{14} & I_1 & I_0 & \mathbf{0}
  \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
  G_{64 \times 128} = \big[\; I_{64} \;\big|\; W_{64 \times 64} \;\big].
\end{equation}
In \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_ul\_short\_ldpc\_dec.sv}, we fold each decoding iteration across \textbf{4 time-multiplexed phases (16 check rows per clock cycle)} using the \textbf{Scaled Offset Min-Sum (Soft-OMS)} algorithm. For check node $m$ and neighboring variable nodes $n \in \mathcal{N}(m)$, the check-to-variable extrinsic message $L_{m \to n}^{(t)}$ and updated variable posterior $Q_n^{(t)}$ are:
\begin{align}
  L_{m \to n}^{(t)} &= \left(\prod_{n' \in \mathcal{N}(m)\setminus\{n\}} \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(q_{n' \to m}^{(t-1)}\big)\right) \cdot \max\!\left(\min_{n' \in \mathcal{N}(m)\setminus\{n\}} \big|q_{n' \to m}^{(t-1)}\big| - \beta_{\text{oms}},\; 0\right), \\
  Q_n^{(t)} &= \ell_n^{\text{ch}} + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}(n)} L_{m \to n}^{(t)}, \qquad \text{Early Stop} \iff \mathbf{s}^{(t)} = \hat{\mathbf{c}}^{(t)} H^T = \mathbf{0} \pmod 2.
\end{align}
This 4-phase folded pipeline reduces the combinational critical path to just \textbf{11 logic levels ($110.22\,\text{MHz}$ True $F_{\max}$ on Zynq-7010, $203.38\,\text{MHz}$ on Kintex UltraScale)} while achieving a $+6.3\,\text{dB}$ coding gain over uncoded BPSK at $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Downlink Dual-Basis $\text{RS}(255,223)$ Codec ($t=16$ Berlekamp-Massey, Chien \& Forney)}
The downlink Reed-Solomon outer codec (\texttt{ccsds\_dl\_rs255\_223\_enc.sv} and \texttt{ccsds\_dl\_rs255\_223\_dec.sv}) operates over the symmetric 32-root generator polynomial mandated by CCSDS 131.0-B-5 \cite{ccsds131}:
\begin{equation}
  g_{\text{RS}}(x) = \prod_{j=112}^{143}\!\left(x - \alpha^{11j}\right) = \sum_{k=0}^{32} g_k x^k, \qquad \alpha^{11 \times 112} = \alpha^{212}, \dots, \alpha^{11 \times 143} = \alpha^{43}.
\end{equation}
In the Ground EGSE persona (\texttt{ROLE\_MODE = 1/2}), \texttt{ccsds\_dl\_rs255\_223\_dec.sv} maps each incoming dual-basis byte into standard basis via $T_\alpha^{-1}$ and executes a 4-stage hardware algebraic decoding pipeline capable of correcting any $t \le 16$ symbol errors per 255-byte codeword:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Horner Syndrome Evaluation (\texttt{ST\_ACCUM}):} Evaluates all 32 syndromes $S_k = R(\alpha^{11(112+k)})$ for $k \in [0..31]$ on the fly as bytes stream in ($S_k \leftarrow (S_k \otimes \alpha^{11(112+k)}) \oplus r_i$). If $\bigvee_{k=0}^{31} S_k == 0$, the codeword is emitted with zero correction latency.
  \item \textbf{8-Cycle Unrolled Berlekamp-Massey Solver (\texttt{RS\_DEC\_SOLVE\_BM}):} Solves the key equation $\Lambda(z) S(z) \equiv \Omega(z) \pmod{z^{32}}$ for the degree-$\nu$ ($\nu \le 16$) error-locator polynomial $\Lambda(z) = 1 + \Lambda_1 z + \dots + \Lambda_\nu z^\nu$ in just 8 clock cycles (4 Berlekamp-Massey discrepancy updates $d_n = S_n \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^{L} \Lambda_i \otimes S_{n-i}$ per clock).
  \item \textbf{Forney Error-Evaluator Polynomial (\texttt{RS\_DEC\_SOLVE\_OMEGA}):} Computes the degree-15 evaluator polynomial $\Omega(z) = S(z)\Lambda(z) \bmod z^{32}$ in 2 clock cycles.
  \item \textbf{Pipelined Chien Search \& Forney Correction (\texttt{DEC\_CHIEN / DEC\_FORNEY}):} Sweeps $x_i = \alpha^{-(254-i)}$ across the buffered codeword. Whenever $\Lambda(x_i^{-1}) = \Lambda_{\text{even}}(x_i^{-1}) \oplus \Lambda_{\text{odd}}(x_i^{-1}) == 0$ (where $\Lambda_{\text{odd}}(x_i^{-1}) = x_i^{-1}\Lambda'(x_i^{-1})$ in characteristic 2), the exact error magnitude $e_i$ is computed via Forney's formula with root-offset compensation $\alpha^{11 \times 112} = \alpha^{212}$ and XOR-healed back into dual basis:
  \begin{equation}
    e_i = \frac{x_i^{112\times 11}\,\Omega(x_i^{-1})}{\Lambda'(x_i^{-1})} = \frac{x_i^{212}\,\Omega(x_i^{-1})}{x_i\,\Lambda_{\text{odd}}(x_i^{-1})}, \qquad \hat{c}_{\text{dual},i} = r_{\text{dual},i} \oplus T_\alpha(e_i).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Zero-Trust Space Data Link Security (SDLS), COP-1 ARQ \& Radiation Mitigation}

Having corrected channel bit errors in Chapter~4, the receiver must verify cryptographic authenticity and frame sequence order without exposing the spacecraft to Denial-of-Service lockouts or Galois/Counter Mode (GCM) Initialization Vector (IV) reuse. This chapter formulates the \textbf{Two-Phase \texttt{FARM-1}/\texttt{SDLS} Speculative Commit/Rollback Protocol}, derives the \textbf{domain-separated 96-bit \texttt{AES-128-GCM} IV and 4-bit/clock $GF(2^{128})$ \texttt{GHASH} engine}, and analyzes the 3x Triple-Modular Redundancy (TMR) voters.

\subsection{Two-Phase \texttt{FARM-1} \& \texttt{SDLS} Speculative Commit Proof}
Let $V(R) \in [0..255]$ denote the committed receiver sequence counter in \texttt{ccsds\_farm1\_tmr.sv} and let $\text{Seq}_{\text{SA}}[v] \in [0..2^{32}-1]$ denote the monotonic anti-replay counter for Virtual Channel $v \in [0..63]$ in \texttt{ccsds\_sdls\_engine.sv}. Rather than advancing $V(R)$ before authentication, the receiver executes a two-phase transition across \texttt{clk\_tc\_safe} and \texttt{clk\_sys}:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
  \item \textbf{Phase~A (\texttt{clk\_tc\_safe}):} \texttt{ccsds\_farm1\_tmr.sv} verifies $N(S) == V(R)$ while holding $V(R)$ frozen, and streams the frame into the speculative rollback FIFO (\texttt{u\_rx\_commit\_cdc\_fifo}).
  \item \textbf{Phase~B (\texttt{clk\_sys}):} \texttt{ccsds\_sdls\_engine.sv} verifies $\text{Seq}_{\text{rx}} > \text{Seq}_{\text{SA}}[v]$ and $T_{\text{calc}} == T_{\text{rx}}$ before asserting either \texttt{sdls\_commit\_pulse} or \texttt{sdls\_rollback\_pulse}.
\end{enumerate}
This decoupled transition guarantees two formal security invariants:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Spoof Immunity ($T_{\text{calc}} \ne T_{\text{rx}}$ or $\text{Seq}_{\text{rx}} \le \text{Seq}_{\text{SA}}[v]$):} \texttt{sdls\_rollback\_pulse} rewinds the speculative FIFO write pointer and leaves both $V(R)$ and $\text{Seq}_{\text{SA}}[v]$ invariant. An adversary injecting arbitrary CRC-valid frames cannot advance $V(R)$ or trigger \texttt{FARM-1} Lockout.
  \item \textbf{Out-of-Sequence Rejection ($N(S) \ne V(R)$):} Frames outside the current expectation are rejected in Phase~A before reaching \texttt{ccsds\_sdls\_engine.sv}, ensuring that dropped frames or Go-Back-$N$ retransmissions never prematurely advance $\text{Seq}_{\text{SA}}[v]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Domain-Separated AES-128-GCM IV \& $GF(2^{128})$ \texttt{GHASH}}
In NIST SP 800-38D Galois/Counter Mode (\texttt{AES-128-GCM}), encrypting two distinct frames under the same 128-bit key $K$ and identical 96-bit Initialization Vector ($\text{IV}_{96}$) leaks the XOR of their plaintexts and allows polynomial root recovery of the hash subkey $H = \text{AES}_K(0^{128})$ (the GCM ``Forbidden Attack'').

To guarantee global $\text{IV}_{96}$ uniqueness across all 64 Virtual Channels and across multiple spacecraft sharing a mission key, \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_sdls\_engine.sv} (line 206, \texttt{format\_aes\_ctr\_block}) constructs the 128-bit counter block $J_j = \{\text{IV}_{96},\; \texttt{16'h0000},\; \text{blk\_idx}[15:0]\}$ using the 16-bit Security Parameter Index ($\text{SPI}[15:0]$) and 32-bit monotonic sequence number ($\text{Seq}[31:0]$) from the 6-byte CCSDS 355.0-B-2 Security Header, framed by fixed domain constants (\texttt{16'hCC5D} and \texttt{32'h5344\_4C53} = ASCII \texttt{"SDLS"}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \text{IV}_{96} &= \big\{\,\texttt{16'hCC5D},\; \underbrace{\text{SCID}[9:0],\; \text{VCID}[5:0]}_{\text{Domain-Separated }\text{SPI}[15:0]},\; \text{Seq}[31:0],\; \texttt{32'h5344\_4C53}\,\big\}, \\
  J_0 &= \big\{\,\text{IV}_{96},\; \texttt{32'h0000\_0001}\,\big\}, \qquad J_j = \big\{\,\text{IV}_{96},\; \texttt{16'h0000},\; (j+1)[15:0]\,\big\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Because each Virtual Channel $v \in [0..63]$ on Spacecraft $\text{SCID} \in [0..1023]$ is provisioned in the 64-entry SA RAM with its unique domain-separated $\text{SPI} = \{\text{SCID}[9:0], \text{VCID}[5:0]\}$ (see Volume~I, Section~6.3), the tuple $(\text{SCID}, \text{VCID}, \text{Seq})$ is injectively embedded inside $\text{IV}_{96}$, making cross-VC or cross-spacecraft IV collisions mathematically impossible.

To avoid instantiating separate AES cores for key expansion, counter-stream encryption, and hash-subkey derivation, \texttt{ccsds\_sdls\_engine.sv} time-multiplexes a single 10-round AES-128 datapath across three phases per frame:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Phase 1 (Hash Subkey $H$):} Evaluates $H = \text{AES}_K(0^{128})$ in 10 clock cycles.
  \item \textbf{Phase 2 (Initial Counter Mask $E_K(J_0)$):} Evaluates $E_K(J_0)$ in 10 clock cycles and feeds the 48-bit Security Header $\{\text{SPI}[15:0], \text{Seq}[31:0]\}$ padded with $0^{80}$ into the \texttt{GHASH} accumulator as Additional Authenticated Data ($A$).
  \item \textbf{Phase 3 (Payload Counter Stream \& 4-Bit/Clock $GF(2^{128})$ \texttt{GHASH}):} For each 128-bit payload block $j \ge 1$, XORs $E_K(J_j)$ with the plaintext/ciphertext and updates $Y_j = (Y_{j-1} \oplus C_j) \cdot H$ over $GF(2^{128}) \cong \mathbb{F}_2[x]/\langle 1 + x + x^2 + x^7 + x^{128}\rangle$ (\texttt{ghash\_step\_4bit}, 4 bits per clock = 32 clocks per 128-bit block, requiring \textbf{1 LUT6 logic level and 0 DSP slices}). After the length block $\text{len}(A)_{64} \Vert \text{len}(C)_{64}$, the 128-bit authentication tag is $T = Y_m \oplus E_K(J_0)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Triple-Modular Redundancy (3x TMR) \& Single-Cycle Auto-Scrubbing}
Every state register in \texttt{ccsds\_farm1\_tmr.sv}, \texttt{ccsds\_hcpd\_ctrl.sv}, and \texttt{ccsds\_axi\_lite\_csr.sv} is triplicated across three preserved flip-flop banks (\texttt{*\_a\_q}, \texttt{*\_b\_q}, \texttt{*\_c\_q} guarded by \texttt{(* syn\_preserve = 1, dont\_touch = "true" *)}) and voted combinationally on every cycle:
\begin{equation}
  v = \operatorname{vote3}(a, b, c) = (a \land b) \lor (b \land c) \lor (a \land c), \qquad
  e_{\text{seu}} = (a \oplus b) \lor (b \oplus c).
\end{equation}
Whenever an ionizing particle flips a bit in any replica ($e_{\text{seu}} \ne 0$), $\operatorname{vote3}$ masks the fault with zero output latency, writes the clean voted state $v$ back into all three replicas on the next clock edge, increments \texttt{cnt\_tmr\_seu\_corr\_q} (\texttt{0x18[31:16]}), and pulses \texttt{edac\_scrub\_req\_out}.

\section{All-Digital Baseband RF Modem DSP \& Regenerative Pseudo-Noise Ranging}

Moving from the protocol and security planes down to the physical waveform boundary, \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_phy\_modem.sv} and \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_pn\_ranging.sv} synthesize and demodulate complex baseband I/Q sample streams in the $120\,\text{MHz}$ \texttt{clk\_adc\_iq} domain. \subsection{Multiplier-Free CSD 17-Tap 4-SPS SRRC Filter ($\alpha = 0.35$)}
Per CCSDS 401.0-B-32 \cite{ccsds401}, baseband pulse shaping uses a Square-Root Raised-Cosine (SRRC) transfer function with roll-off $\alpha = 0.35$ across $t \in [-2T_s, +2T_s]$ at $N_{\text{sps}} = 4$ Samples Per Symbol ($17$ taps $n \in [0..16]$, center peak $n = 8$):
\begin{equation}
  h_{\text{SRRC}}(t) = \frac{\sin\!\left[\pi \frac{t}{T_s}(1-\alpha)\right] + 4\alpha \frac{t}{T_s}\cos\!\left[\pi \frac{t}{T_s}(1+\alpha)\right]}{\pi \frac{t}{T_s}\left[1 - \left(4\alpha \frac{t}{T_s}\right)^2\right]}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating $h_{\text{SRRC}}\big((n-8)T_s/4\big)$ at $\alpha = 0.35$ and quantizing to 11-bit signed fixed-point precision (\texttt{csd\_srrc\_tap} in \texttt{rtl/ccsds\_phy\_modem.sv}) yields the symmetric impulse response:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  h_{\text{SRRC}}[0..16] = \Big\{&+29,\; -11,\; -69,\; -97,\; -43,\; \mathbf{+106,\; +312,\; +491,\; +562,\; +491,\; +312,\; +106}, \\
                                   &-43,\; -97,\; -69,\; -11,\; +29\Big\} / 2048.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Two structural properties govern the hardware realization of Equation~(6.2):
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
  \item \textbf{Strictly Positive Main Lobe ($n \in [5..11]$):} Because the first zero-crossing occurs at $|t| \approx 0.89 T_s$, all seven central main-lobe taps ($+106, +312, +491, +562, +491, +312, +106$) are strictly positive, yielding $>46\,\text{dB}$ Signal-to-ISI ratio at the Nyquist strobes $n \equiv 0 \pmod 4$.
  \item \textbf{2-Level Binary CSD Adder Trees (\texttt{0 DSP Slices}):} Pre-folding symmetric taps ($s_k = x[k] + x[16-k]$ for $k \in [0..7]$ and $s_8 = x[8]$) decomposes the 9 unique coefficients into 2-level shift-and-add trees:
  \begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    +29\,s_0 &= (32s_0 - 4s_0) + s_0,           & -11\,s_1  &= (4s_1 - 16s_1) + s_1, \\
    -69\,s_2 &= -64s_2 - (4s_2 + s_2),          & -97\,s_3  &= (32s_3 - 128s_3) - s_3, \\
    -43\,s_4 &= (4s_4 - 32s_4) - (16s_4 - s_4), & +106\,s_5 &= (128s_5 - 16s_5) - (4s_5 + 2s_5), \\
    +312\,s_6 &= (256s_6 + 64s_6) - 8s_6,       & +491\,s_7 &= (512s_7 - 16s_7) - (4s_7 + s_7), \\
    +562\,s_8 &= (512s_8 + 32s_8) + (16s_8 + 2s_8). & &
  \end{aligned}
  \end{equation}
  On the transmit side, ternary symbol inputs $s \in \{0, \pm 1\}$ allow \texttt{tx\_pair12\_lut6} and \texttt{tx\_pair34\_lut6} to evaluate pairs of polyphase taps directly inside single 6-input LUTs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3-Stage Pipelined 4-SPS Gardner Symbol Timing Recovery}
To recover symbol timing across arbitrary fractional clock offsets $\tau \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ samples per symbol at $120\,\text{MHz}$, \texttt{ccsds\_phy\_modem.sv} implements a 3-stage pipelined \textbf{Gardner Timing Error Detector (TED)} (\cite{ccsds401}) using the on-time strobe sample $y_I(k T_s), y_Q(k T_s)$ and the mid-symbol transition sample $y_I((k - 1/2)T_s), y_Q((k - 1/2)T_s)$:
\begin{equation}
  e_{\text{TED}}(k) = y_I\!\left(k - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Big[y_I(k) - y_I(k-1)\Big] + y_Q\!\left(k - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Big[y_Q(k) - y_Q(k-1)\Big].
\end{equation}
Because $e_{\text{TED}}(k)$ is a real inner product $\operatorname{Re}\{y^*(k-1/2)[y(k)-y(k-1)]\}$, any common carrier phase rotation $e^{j\theta}$ cancels out identically, allowing symbol timing to lock before carrier phase acquisition completes.

\subsection{Differential Cross-Product FLL-Assisted Costas Loop}
During a Low-Earth Orbit horizon-to-horizon pass ($h = 550\text{--}600\,\text{km}$), radial velocity induces up to $\pm 50.8\,\text{kHz}$ S-Band ($\pm 178.1\,\text{kHz}$ X-Band) carrier Doppler shift, exceeding the linear pull-in range of a classic 2nd-order Costas phase detector. In \texttt{ccsds\_phy\_modem.sv}, the carrier recovery loop combines a wide-pull-in \textbf{Sign-Quantized Differential Cross-Product Frequency-Locked Loop (FLL)} discriminator $e_{\text{FLL}}(k)$ with a decision-directed \textbf{Costas Phase Detector} $e_{\text{PLL}}(k)$:
\begin{align}
  e_{\text{FLL}}(k) &= \operatorname{sgn}(I_{k-1})\,Q_k - \operatorname{sgn}(Q_{k-1})\,I_k \propto |A|^2 \sin(\Delta\omega\, T_s), \\
  e_{\text{PLL}}(k) &= \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{sgn}(I_k)\,Q_k & (\text{BPSK / PCM-PM}), \\
    \operatorname{sgn}(I_k)\,Q_k - \operatorname{sgn}(Q_k)\,I_k & (\text{QPSK / OQPSK}),
  \end{cases} \\
  \text{FCW}_{\text{rx}}(k) &= \text{FCW}_{\text{rx}}(k-1) + K_I e_{\text{PLL}}(k) + K_{\text{FLL}} e_{\text{FLL}}(k).
\end{align}
Because $e_{\text{FLL}}(k)$ measures the phase rotation between consecutive samples $k-1$ and $k$, it provides an unambiguous frequency error signal across $\pm 640$ NCO frequency steps, rapidly slewing $\text{FCW}_{\text{rx}}$ into coherent Costas phase lock.

\subsection{Wideband Regenerative \texttt{T2B}/\texttt{T4B} Pseudo-Noise Ranging Correlator}
Per CCSDS 414.1-B-3 \cite{ccsds414}, ground stations measure two-way spacecraft slant range to sub-meter precision by transmitting a composite Pseudo-Noise (PN) sequence of period $L = 2 \times 7 \times 11 \times 15 \times 19 \times 23 = 1{,}009{,}470$ chips formed by weighted majority voting of six coprime LFSR component sequences $C_1(t), \dots, C_6(t) \in \{-1, +1\}$:
\begin{align}
  \text{T4B}(t) &= \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(4\,C_1(t) + C_2(t) + C_3(t) + C_4(t) + C_5(t) + C_6(t)\big), \\
  \text{T2B}(t) &= \operatorname{sgn}\!\big(2\,C_1(t) + C_2(t) + C_3(t) + C_4(t) + C_5(t) + C_6(t)\big).
\end{align}
Inside \texttt{ccsds\_pn\_ranging.sv}, six parallel correlator banks exploit the mutual coprimality of the component lengths to evaluate only $2 + 7 + 11 + 15 + 19 + 23 = \mathbf{77\text{ hypotheses}}$ in parallel—reducing acquisition complexity by $13{,}110\times$ compared to a serial search over $1{,}009{,}470$ chips.

\subsection{Five RF Cascade Placement Proofs \& NASA/ECSS Vacuum Power Derating}
Referring to the upright 4-zone S-Band architecture in Volume~I (\textbf{Figure~7.2}), five quantitative RF constraints govern the placement and power derating of every component in the chain:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{3pt}
  \item \textbf{Why RF Isolator \texttt{\#3} Must Sit \emph{Upstream} of Bi-Directional Coupler \texttt{\#2} (\texttt{PA $\to$ \#3 Isolator $\to$ \#2 Coupler $\to$ \#4 Diplexer}):} A bi-directional coupler (\textbf{RFFE \texttt{\#2}}, \href{https://www.minicircuits.com/pdfs/SYBD-20-272HP+.pdf}{\textbf{Mini-Circuits \texttt{SYBD-20-272HP+}}}, $19.8\,\text{dB}$ coupling, $25\,\text{dB}$ directivity) can only measure reflected power coming from its output port (Port~2). If the RF isolator (\textbf{RFFE \texttt{\#3}}, \href{https://www.rfmw.com/products/detail/rfsl2848-rf-circulator-isolator/470641/}{\textbf{RFCI \texttt{RFSL2848}}}, $25\,\text{dB}$ reverse isolation, $20\,\text{W}$ load) were placed between the coupler and the diplexer, any reflected wave $\Gamma_{\text{ant}}$ from a jammed or undeployed antenna would be attenuated by $25\,\text{dB}$ inside the isolator before reaching Coupler Port~2, making the antenna fault invisible at Coupler Port~4! Placing \textbf{Isolator \texttt{\#3} immediately after the \texttt{SKY66294-11} PA} and \textbf{Coupler \texttt{\#2} downstream between the Isolator and Diplexer \texttt{\#4}} ensures that Coupler Port~4 senses 100\% of the true antenna/diplexer reflection ($P_{\text{rev,Port4}} = P_{\text{fwd}} + 20\log_{10}|\Gamma_{\text{ant}}| - 19.8\,\text{dB}$), while the reverse wave exiting Coupler Port~1 is absorbed harmlessly in the isolator's $20\,\text{W}$ termination load before reaching the PA.
  \item \textbf{Why $<500\,\text{ns}$ PA Protection Requires an Analog Window Comparator (\texttt{TLV4041R1}) Ahead of \texttt{AUXADC\_0}:} Under a total antenna open/short ($\Gamma_{\text{ant}} = 1.0$) at $+33.7\,\text{dBm}$ forward power, $+13.9\,\text{dBm}$ reaches Coupler Port~4 ($-6.1\,\text{dBm}$ after the $20\,\text{dB}$ \texttt{YAT-20+} pad), driving the \href{https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ad8362.pdf}{\textbf{Analog Devices \texttt{AD8362}}} True-RMS detector output \texttt{VOUT} above $1.45\,\text{V}$ within $420\,\text{ns}$. Because polling \texttt{ADRV9002} \texttt{AUXADC\_0} over a $20\,\text{MHz}$ SPI bus requires $\sim 4.8\,\mu\text{s}$, routing \texttt{AD8362} \texttt{VOUT} into a fast open-drain comparator (\textbf{TI \texttt{TLV4041R1}}, threshold $V_{\text{th}} = 1.15\,\text{V} \iff \text{VSWR} = 3.0:1$, delay $45\,\text{ns}$) that directly clears the hardware \texttt{PA\_EN} latch guarantees PA bias cutoff in \textbf{$t_{\text{trip}} = 420\,\text{ns} + 45\,\text{ns} = \mathbf{465\,\text{ns}}$ ($< 0.5\,\mu\text{s}$)}, while \texttt{AUXADC\_0} logs continuous return-loss telemetry in software.
  \item \textbf{NASA EEE-INST-002 / ECSS-Q-ST-30-11C 50\% Vacuum Power Derating on Flight Path~A (\texttt{CTS UPD215A}):} In space vacuum ($<10^{-5}\,\text{Torr}$), convective air cooling is absent and high RF electric fields inside miniature ceramic resonators risk localized dielectric heating and multipaction. Both \textbf{NASA EEE-INST-002 (Section M4, Passive RF Devices)} and \textbf{ECSS-Q-ST-30-11C} mandate a $\ge 50\%$ CW RF power derating factor ($P_{\text{op,max}} \le 0.50 \times P_{\text{rated}}$):
  \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
    \item \emph{Flight Path~B (\texttt{D0421552} / \texttt{AE2067} Cavity Diplexer, $P_{\text{rated}} = 20.0\,\text{W}$):} The 50\% derated ceiling is $10.0\,\text{W}$ ($+40.0\,\text{dBm}$). Operating the \texttt{SKY66294-11} PA at $\mathbf{+34.00\,\text{dBm}}$ ($2.51\,\text{W}$ nominal) or $\mathbf{+34.50\,\text{dBm}}$ ($2.82\,\text{W}$ peak OQPSK DPD) injects $2.33\text{--}2.62\,\text{W}$ into the diplexer—just \textbf{$11.6\text{--}13.1\%$ of its $20\,\text{W}$ rating}, providing $>6.8\,\text{dB}$ of vacuum power margin.
    \item \emph{Flight Path~A (\texttt{CTS UPD215A} Monolithic SMT Diplexer, $P_{\text{rated}} = 3.0\,\text{W}$):} The 50\% vacuum-derated ceiling at the diplexer Tx input is \textbf{$1.50\,\text{W}$ ($+31.76\,\text{dBm}$)}. Therefore, when populated with the all-SMT \texttt{UPD215A} (Path~A), software sets \texttt{ADRV9002} \texttt{TX1} output to $-3.40\,\text{dBm}$ so the \texttt{SKY66294-11} PA operates at a derated linear output of \textbf{$\mathbf{+31.50\,\text{dBm}}$ ($1.41\,\text{W}$)}, injecting \textbf{$+31.03\,\text{dBm}$ ($1.27\,\text{W} = 42.3\%$ of the $3.0\,\text{W}$ rating)} into the \texttt{UPD215A} diplexer and delivering $\mathbf{+29.30\,\text{dBm}}$ ($0.85\,\text{W}$) to the antenna port while still closing the $2.0\,\text{Mbps}$ $600\,\text{km}$ LEO downlink with \textbf{$+9.39\,\text{dB}$ net margin}!
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pre-PA Tx Bandpass Filtering (\texttt{RFFE \#1}) to Prevent Uplink Receiver Desensitization:} The \texttt{ADRV9002} \texttt{TX1} DAC thermal noise floor in the $2025\text{--}2110\,\text{MHz}$ Rx band is $\approx -154.5\,\text{dBm/Hz}$. Placing \textbf{RFFE \texttt{\#1}} (\texttt{Anatech AE2250B10113} or \texttt{Mini-Circuits BFCN-2275+}, $>25\text{--}35\,\text{dB}$ rejection at $2025\text{--}2110\,\text{MHz}$) \emph{before} the $+36.5\,\text{dB}$ PA suppresses amplified Tx noise at the LNA input below $-204\,\text{dBm/Hz}$ ($>30\,\text{dB}$ below the $-174\,\text{dBm/Hz}$ thermal floor).
  \item \textbf{Post-LNA Interstage Rx Bandpass Filter (\texttt{RFFE \#5}) \& PIN Limiter (\texttt{\#7B}):} Placing the \href{https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/E8035E0D44534E2F81C778F84AF03796.pdf}{\textbf{Skyworks \texttt{SKY67153-396LF}}} LNA ($0.35\,\text{dB}$ NF, $+20.5\,\text{dB}$ gain, $+19\,\text{dBm}$ IIP3) immediately after the diplexer locks in a sub-$2\,\text{dB}$ system Noise Figure, while placing the \textbf{Sangshin \texttt{MBP76RC2067S85A}} BPF ($1.20\,\text{dB}$ IL, $65\,\text{dB}$ typ Tx rejection) and \textbf{Skyworks \texttt{SKY16602-632LF}} PIN limiter ($0.50\,\text{dB}$ IL) \emph{after} the LNA suppresses residual Tx leakage at \texttt{ADRV9002} \texttt{RX1A} below $-68\,\text{dBm}$ ($>119\,\text{dB}$ total Tx rejection) at a Friis NF penalty of only $+0.02\,\text{dB}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{First-Principles Friis Noise Temperature Derivation \& $600\,\text{km}$ LEO Link Budget}
Let $L_{\text{pre,dB}} = L_{\text{coax,dB}} + L_{\text{dip,rx,dB}}$ denote the passive loss preceding the LNA: $L_{\text{pre,dB}} = 0.20 + 0.60 = \mathbf{0.80\,\text{dB}}$ ($F_1 = 1.2023$) for \textbf{Path~B} and $L_{\text{pre,dB}} = 0.15 + 1.50 = \mathbf{1.65\,\text{dB}}$ ($F_1 = 1.4622$) for \textbf{Path~A}. With $NF_{\text{LNA}} = 0.35\,\text{dB}$ ($F_{\text{LNA}} = 1.0839$, $G_{\text{LNA}} = 10^{2.05} = 112.20$) and post-LNA loss $L_{\text{post,dB}} = 1.20 + 0.50 = 1.70\,\text{dB}$ ahead of \texttt{ADRV9002} \texttt{RX1A} ($NF_{\text{ADRV}} = 11.50\,\text{dB}$ including the \texttt{TCM2-33WX+} \texttt{T201} balun, $F_{\text{post}} = 10^{1.32} = 20.893$), Friis' formula gives:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  NF_{\text{rx}} &= L_{\text{pre,dB}} + 10\log_{10}\!\Big(F_{\text{LNA}} + \frac{F_{\text{post}} - 1}{G_{\text{LNA}}}\Big)
                  = L_{\text{pre,dB}} + 10\log_{10}(1.0839 + 0.1773) = \mathbf{L_{\text{pre,dB}} + 1.01\,\text{dB}} \\[2pt]

  \textbf{Path A:}\quad & NF_{\text{rx}} = \mathbf{2.66\text{--}2.81\,\text{dB}}, \quad T_{\text{rx}} = 264\,\text{K}, \quad T_{\text{sys}} = 514\,\text{K}\;(27.11\,\text{dBK}), \quad G/T = \mathbf{-24.11\,\text{dB/K}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Space-Qualification Engineering: TID, SEE, Outgassing \& Class-S/Level-1 Alternatives}
Deploying a COTS-derived S-Band transceiver on a LEO smallsat ($h = 500\text{--}600\,\text{km}$, 3--5 year mission life, Total Ionizing Dose $\text{TID} \approx 5\text{--}15\,\text{krad(Si)}$ behind $2.5\,\text{mm}$ Al chassis shielding) or scaling to a longer-duration institutional mission requires a structured parts-qualification and radiation-mitigation plan:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Single-Event Latch-Up (SEL) \& TID Screening:} The \textbf{Lattice MachXO5-NX (\texttt{LFMXO5-100T})} is fabricated on 28nm Fully-Depleted Silicon-On-Insulator (FD-SOI) technology, where the buried oxide layer eliminates parasitic thyristor structures ($>80\,\text{MeV}\cdot\text{cm}^2/\text{mg}$ SEL immunity, $\text{TID} > 50\,\text{krad(Si)}$). The III-V GaAs/InGaP active RF stages (\textbf{\texttt{SKY66294-11}} HBT PA and \textbf{\texttt{SKY67153-396LF}} pHEMT LNA) are majority-carrier III-V devices inherently immune to CMOS latch-up ($\text{TID} > 100\,\text{krad}$). For the CMOS \textbf{\texttt{ADRV9002}} and \textbf{\texttt{ADP5056}}, lot acceptance proton/heavy-ion SEE screening per \textbf{NASA EEE-INST-002 Level~2/3} is paired with an upstream current-limiting latch-up protection switch (\textbf{TI \texttt{TPS25947}} or rad-tolerant \textbf{Microchip \texttt{LX7712}}) that cycles the $5\,\text{V}$ sub-rail within $50\,\mu\text{s}$ if supply current exceeds $135\%$ of nominal.
  \item \textbf{Thermal-Vacuum Outgassing (ASTM E595 / ECSS-Q-ST-70-02C) \& Pure-Lead Solder Reflow:} All organic-packaged QFN/BGA ICs undergo $125^\circ\text{C}$ 24-hour vacuum bake-out prior to conformal staking with space-grade epoxy (\textbf{3M Scotch-Weld \texttt{EC-2216 B/A Gray}} or \textbf{NuSil \texttt{CV-2946}}, Total Mass Loss $\text{TML} < 1.0\%$, Collected Volatile Condensable Material $\text{CVCM} < 0.10\%$), assembled with eutectic tin-lead (\texttt{Sn63Pb37}) solder to prevent tin-whisker growth in vacuum.
  \item \textbf{Drop-In Class-S / Level-1 Radiation-Hardened Upgrade Path:} For GEO or deep-space missions ($\text{TID} \ge 100\,\text{krad(Si)}$), the vendor-agnostic \texttt{ccsds\_sat\_ttc\_top} RTL targets \textbf{Microchip RT PolarFire (\texttt{RTPF500ZT})} or \textbf{AMD/Xilinx Space-Grade Kintex UltraScale (\texttt{XQRKU060})}, while the \texttt{ADP5056} PMIC is replaced by hermetically sealed QML-V point-of-load regulators (\textbf{Renesas/Intersil \texttt{ISL70002SEH}}) and ultra-low-noise linear regulators (\textbf{ADI \texttt{RH1086MK}} / radiation-characterized \textbf{\texttt{LT3045}}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Statistical BER/FER Waterfall Performance \& Orbital Pass Physics}

While Volume~I, Chapter~8 provides the practical regression execution commands and sign-off summary table, this final chapter analyzes the statistical coding-gain curves from our 23-point $E_b/N_0$ waterfall campaign (\texttt{Stage 10}), explains the mathematical relationship between shortened virtual-fill Reed-Solomon codewords and sample count in the 34,560-trial matrix (\texttt{Stage 11}), and examines the continuous-time orbital pass dynamics in \texttt{Stage 12}.

\subsection{23-Point Statistical \texorpdfstring{$E_b/N_0$}{Eb/N0} Waterfall Analysis (\texttt{Stage 10})}
To characterize the coding threshold of every uplink and downlink codec across the full transition from channel breakdown to error-free operation, \textbf{Figure~8.1} plots the 23-point statistical Monte Carlo sweep ($E_b/N_0 \in [0.0, 11.0]\,\text{dB}$ in $0.5\,\text{dB}$ steps). At a target post-FEC Bit Error Rate of $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$, the measured curves confirm theoretical coding gains:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Uncoded BPSK Baseline (CCSDS 401.0-B-32):} Follows $\text{BER} = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{E_b/N_0})$, requiring $E_b/N_0 = 10.54\,\text{dB}$ at $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$.
  \item \textbf{Uplink Expurgated $\text{BCH}(63,56)$ SEC-DED:} Achieves $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$ at $E_b/N_0 = 8.80\,\text{dB}$ ($+1.74\,\text{dB}$ coding gain) while providing deterministic single-error correction and double-error codeblock rejection.
  \item \textbf{Uplink 4-Phase $\text{Short-LDPC}(128,64)$ Soft-OMS (16 Iterations):} Achieves $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$ at $E_b/N_0 = \mathbf{4.20\,\text{dB}}$ ($+6.34\,\text{dB}$ coding gain over uncoded BPSK and $+4.60\,\text{dB}$ over $\text{BCH}(63,56)$).
  \item \textbf{Downlink Soft Viterbi ($K=7, r=1/2$) vs.\ Concatenated $\text{RS}(255,223)$ + Viterbi:} Standalone soft-decision Viterbi reaches $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$ at $5.40\,\text{dB}$; adding the outer $t=16$ Berlekamp-Massey $\text{RS}(255,223)$ decoder ($I=4$) creates a steep waterfall cliff at $E_b/N_0 = \mathbf{2.90\,\text{dB}}$ ($+7.64\,\text{dB}$ net coding gain), while AR4JA quasi-cyclic LDPC reaches $\text{BER} = 10^{-6}$ at $\mathbf{1.90\,\text{dB}}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Open-Loop Waterfall Breakdown (\texttt{Stage 10}) vs.\ Rated-SNR Sign-Off (\texttt{Stage 11})}
Comparing the open-loop waterfall sweep in \textbf{Figure~8.1} (\texttt{Stage 10}) with the 34,560-trial full-chip matrix in \texttt{Stage 11} highlights how the two campaigns complement each other:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2.5pt}
  \item \textbf{Operating SNR Regime:} \texttt{Stage 10} intentionally sweeps down into the negative-margin breakdown region ($E_b/N_0 \in [0, 4]\,\text{dB}$) where channel errors exceed the algebraic correction radius ($t > 16$ bytes in RS or $> 1$ bit in BCH), producing the non-zero $\text{FER} \in [10^{-2}, 1.0]$ points on the left side of \textbf{Figure~8.1B}. Conversely, \texttt{Stage 11} evaluates all 5,760 valid CCSDS configurations at the \textbf{rated mission operating point} ($E_b/N_0 \ge 7.5\,\text{dB}$ on the right side of the waterfall knee in \textbf{Figure~8.1A/B}, combined with deterministic pre-FEC error injection inside the guaranteed correction radius $t_{\text{inj}} \le t_{\max}$). In that regime, every valid codeword must be healed to \textbf{0 post-FEC bit errors ($\text{BER} = 0.0, \text{FER} = 0.0$)}—proving that 23,040 injected channel bit errors across 34,560 bidirectional trials are corrected with zero residual errors.
  \item \textbf{Mathematical Basis of the 39,306,240 Baseband Sample Count:} In \texttt{tb/tb\_stage11\_top\_to\_top\_rf\_link.sv}, rather than transmitting 223-byte unshortened frames ($Q=0$) on all 34,560 trials, the testbench exercises CCSDS 131.0-B-5 \textbf{implicit virtual fill $Q \in [185..205]$} across shortened payload sizes (\texttt{UL} $\in \{8, 14, 28\}\,\text{B}$, \texttt{DL} $\in \{10, 16, 32\}\,\text{B}$) and maintains warm-locked NCO/Gardner loop state across consecutive trials within each worker chunk. Because the $Q$ virtual fill zeros are added algebraically inside the syndrome accumulator without being transmitted over the RF wire, each shortened CADU spans $50..70$ physical bytes ($1,137.3$ average baseband I/Q samples per bidirectional trial $\times 34,560 = 39,306,240$ total samples), testing 100\% of the algebraic finite-field and protocol state transitions in 33 minutes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Continuous-Time LEO Orbital Pass Loopback Dynamics (\texttt{Stage 12})}
Finally, \textbf{Figure~8.2} illustrates the continuous-time physical trajectory simulated in \texttt{tb/tb\_stage12\_space\_channel\_loopback.sv} across a $550\text{--}600\,\text{km}$ LEO horizon-to-horizon pass ($\varepsilon = 10^\circ \to 82^\circ \to 10^\circ$):
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item \textbf{Panels A \& B (Slant Range \& S-Curve Doppler Tracking):} As slant range contracts from $1,932\,\text{km}$ (AOS) to $550\,\text{km}$ (Time of Closest Approach, $t = 0\,\text{s}$) and expands back to $1,932\,\text{km}$ (LOS), radial velocity reverses through zero at maximum negative acceleration, sweeping S-Band Doppler across $\pm 50.8\,\text{kHz}$ ($\pm 178.1\,\text{kHz}$ X-Band). The FLL-assisted PI Costas loop tracks the entire inflexion with zero cycle slips while locking all four turnaround ratios.
  \item \textbf{Panels C \& D (Mid-Pass $-16.5\,\text{dB}$ Rician Deep Fade \& Autonomous COP-1 Go-Back-$N$ Replay):} At $t = -105\,\text{s}$, a simulated atmospheric scintillation / multipath null drops received $E_b/N_0$ by $-16.5\,\text{dB}$ below the $\text{BCH}(63,56)$ and $\text{LDPC}(128,64)$ thresholds, erasing in-flight Telecommand Frame $N(S) = 1$. When Frame $N(S) = 2$ arrives after the fade, spacecraft \texttt{FARM-1} detects the sequence gap ($2 \ne V(R)=1$), rejects the out-of-order frame, and asserts \texttt{CLCW.Retransmit = 1} with $V(R)=1$ on the downlink. Ground \texttt{FOP-1} decodes the downlink \texttt{CLCW}, rewinds $V(S) \leftarrow 1$, and replays Frames $N(S)=1, 2$—restoring 100\% link goodput with \textbf{0 lost or duplicate packets}.
\end{itemize}
